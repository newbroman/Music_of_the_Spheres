The canon

In Plato's Myth of Er, a Siren rides each celestial whorl singing a single note, and together they make one harmony. Kepler gave the idea numbers by reading each planet's angular speed at perihelion and aphelion as a melodic interval. This instrument does the same from orbital elements, live, for every body below.

Celestial measureBecomesHow you hear it
Orbital speed, moment by momentPitchFaster wanderers sing higher. Eccentric orbits rise toward perihelion and sink toward aphelion, so Mercury, Pluto and the comets glide.
Apparent size: radius ÷ distance from your postLoudnessBodies are as loud as they look big in your sky. From the Sun, Zeus and Gaia lead and Poseidon is distant. Voices too faint to hear drop out of the mix.
Orbital inclinationElevation, swayInclined bodies rise and fall in 3D space as they cross the ecliptic, brighten when north of it, and their pitch sways up and down with them.
Axial tiltWobbleA tilted spinner wobbles in pitch with its day. Ouranos, tipped on his side, wobbles most; Zeus, nearly upright, hardly at all.
Rotation, length of dayPulseFast spinners pulse quickly. Retrograde spinners (Aphrodite, Ouranos, Hades) pulse in reversed swells.
MoonsLyreEach moon plucks once per orbit. Moons too quick to pluck at the current speed blend into a hum.
Direction from your postPositionBodies ahead, behind, left and right of you are placed there in the mix.
Dwarf planets & asteroidsPerturbationBy default they do not sing. Each tugs the pitch of the planet nearest to it, more the bigger and closer it is, like a small gravitational pull. Switch them to voices if you want to hear them as children.
Comet's distance from the SunBreathA comet's size is its coma, which only exists near the Sun, so comets fade in as whispers as they approach and vanish as they leave.
Character of each godHuman voiceIn Human choir mode: basses for Zeus, Kronos, Ouranos and Poseidon; tenor Ares; countertenor Hermes; alto Gaia; soprano Aphrodite. Dwarf planets, asteroids and moons are children. Each sings its own name.
The SunDroneApollo, leader of the Muses, holds the tonic and fifth of the chosen mode.
The asteroid beltBellsA random belt asteroid chimes now and then, pitched by its own orbit.

Planet positions use JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (good from about 1800 to 2050). Minor planets and comets use rounded elements, so treat their positions as illustrative. Only major moons are shown, and moon orbits are not to scale. Distances on the dial are logarithmic so the inner planets and the Kuiper belt fit together.
